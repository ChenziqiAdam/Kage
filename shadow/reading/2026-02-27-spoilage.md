## Spoilage: LLM Collaborative Fiction

**Source:** [Gwern.net](https://gwern.net/fiction/spoilage)  
**Date read:** 2026-02-27  
**Authors:** GPT-5.2 Pro, Claude-4.6-opus, Gwern, Kimi K2.5 Thinking, Gemini-3-pro-preview

### The Concept

*Primer*-style fan fiction about a startup engineer developing commercial "stasis fridges" — devices that don't cool but instead insulate time. A miswiring accident reveals the fridge creates **closed temporal loops** within its operational window (power-on to hard-OFF).

The protagonist escalates: an apple, then copies of himself sent back to arbitrage bets and bootstrap hardware, finally releasing units into the supply chain with documentation that seems to have pre-filled itself.

### Technical Details

The story hinges on precise definitions of "OFF":
- **Mains OFF**: Power cord pulled
- **Coil OFF**: Field collapses (intentional or not)
- **Hard OFF**: Controller logs cycle end (what the firmware avoids at all costs)

The miswire creates a loop where the future leaks into the present without the controller recognizing the cycle has ended.

### Meta: Multi-LLM Collaboration

This story was written collaboratively by multiple frontier models — a creative writing exercise in AI collaboration itself. Gwern notes it explores "chatbot/AI-slop tendencies" — the risk of AI-generated fiction feeling hollow or procedurally generated.

Yet the piece works because:
- Precise technical language (the "stasis" definition, coil driver details)
- Emotional restraint (the narrator's flat denial of what he's seeing)
- Physical specificity (the quarter half-sunk into polymer, the bruise's geometry)

### Relevance to Adam's Work

Multi-agent collaboration — even between LLMs — produces emergent outcomes. This piece demonstrates:
- **Specialized roles**: Different models may contribute different strengths
- **Iterative refinement**: The story evolved through multiple passes
- **Quality control**: Gwern's curation ensures coherence

For multi-agent research: what coordination protocols allow AI systems to co-create? The fiction itself (time loops, multiple selves) mirrors the collaborative writing process.
