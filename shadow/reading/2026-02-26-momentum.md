*Source: [Distill.pub — Gabriel Goh, "Why Momentum Really Works" (April 2017)](https://distill.pub/2017/momentum/)*

**The problem:** We think of momentum as a "heavy ball rolling down a hill" — but this cartoon fails to explain *why* momentum works, how much to use, or what speedup to expect.

**The proper model:** Study momentum on convex quadratics f(w) = ½w^T A w - b^T w. In the eigenbasis of curvature matrix A, gradient descent decomposes into n independent 1D problems. Each eigencomponent converges at rate (1 - α·λᵢ)^k where λᵢ is the eigenvalue.

**The core insight — pathological curvature:**
- Different directions have vastly different eigenvalues (poor condition number)
- Steep directions (large λᵢ) need small step-size α to avoid divergence
- Flat directions (small λᵢ) need large α to make progress
- **A single step-size cannot satisfy both**

**What momentum actually does:**
- Expands the permissible step-size range (can use larger α safely)
- Creates acceleration via overshoot-and-correct dynamics
- Provides up to **quadratic speedup** — comparable to FFT, Quicksort, Grover's Algorithm
- Nesterov proved it's optimal in a precise technical sense

**Why the "heavy ball" story fails:** It captures the intuition of persistence but misses:
- The eigenvalue-specific convergence rates
- The expansion of permissible step-sizes
- The optimal tradeoffs between speed and stability

**For Adam's research:**
- Training large models = optimizing in high-dimensional spaces with pathological curvature
- Multi-agent learning involves optimizing multiple objectives — understanding how optimizers handle geometry is fundamental
- The eigenanalysis approach (decomposing into independent problems) generalizes to understanding dynamics in any linear(izable) system
