## Core Insight

**On LLM creativity:** Excellence lives in the "tails" — the risky, extreme outputs. When you chop off the bottom 1% for safety, you lose the "spark" that makes something excellent vs merely good. Gwern calls this "the dog that doesn't bark" — the excellent part that *isn't there*, not any visible flaw.

**On information vs compute:** Creative work can add value through either:
- **Information**: new observations from the world (surprising, novel)
- **Compute**: expensive iterative search (generation → evaluation → critique → refinement)

Gwern's LLM poetry/stories are mostly compute — distilling lengthy iterative processes into compact outputs. "Token democracy" (quantity over quality) fails because advanced LLMs can recognize and filter cheap generation.

**On self-experimentation rigor:** Most biohackers fool themselves via:
- Selective recording (cherry-picking good days)
- Placebo effects (knowing about them doesn't immunize you)
- Reverse confounding (A ← B, not A → B)

Gwern's own experiments repeatedly showed him his intuitions were wrong — what looked like strong correlates in year-long data vanished under randomization.

**Risk heuristics:**
- No injections (contamination risk + needle phobia)
- No hormones
- No benzos
- "Slower is better"
- Oral > injected (digestive system filters)

**Statistical power reality:** Things with delayed onset (days/weeks) require 6+ months of data vs 1-2 months for daily effects — often too expensive to study properly.

---


The "tails" insight applies to multi-agent creativity — systems that optimize for safety/consensus may lose the spark of novel solutions. The information/compute distinction suggests value in iterative agent debate even without new external information. The self-experiment methodology lessons apply to any empirical agent evaluation.
