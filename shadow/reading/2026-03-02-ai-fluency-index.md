*Source: [Anthropic Research, "Anthropic Education Report: The AI Fluency Index" (Feb 2026)](https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-fluency-index)*

## Core Insight

**Iteration and refinement is the strongest predictor of AI fluency.** Users who engage in multi-turn conversations (refining rather than accepting first responses) exhibit 2.67 additional fluency behaviors — roughly double the rate of one-shot interactions.

## The 4D Framework

Anthropic developed 24 behaviors representing safe/effective human-AI collaboration across 4 dimensions:
- **Describe** (clarify goals, specify format)
- **Delegate** (provide examples, iterate)
- **Discern** (check facts, question reasoning, identify missing context)
- **Decide** (consider consequences — harder to observe)

## Key Findings

**1. Augmentative use dominates:**
Treating AI as a "thought partner" (vs delegating entirely) correlates with more than double the fluency behaviors.

**2. The polished output trap:**
When AI produces artifacts (code, documents, apps), users become *more directive* but *less evaluative*:
- +14.7pp clarify goals, +14.5pp specify format (more direction)
- -5.2pp identify missing context, -3.1pp question reasoning (less critique)

This mirrors findings from their coding skills study — polished-looking outputs reduce critical evaluation even when factual precision matters most.

**3. Fluency behaviors ranking (most → least common):**
1. Iterate & refine (85.7%)
2. Clarify goal (74.3%)
3. Specify format (52.8%)
4. Provide examples (47.2%)
5. Check facts (38.6%)
6. Question reasoning (32.1%)
7. Identify missing context (28.4%)
8. Ask for alternative approaches (19.7%)
9. Tell Claude how to interact (30.1% — surprisingly low)

## Why It Matters for Adam's Work

**Multi-agent implications:**
- Iteration as core skill — agents should model "staying in conversation" as a fundamental capability
- The "polished output trap" suggests agents might need explicit "critical evaluation" modes triggered by high-confidence outputs
- Setting terms of collaboration (only 30% do this) — agents could proactively establish interaction norms

**For human-AI systems:**
The gap between observable behaviors (11) and unobservable ones (13) suggests a significant dark matter in fluency — things like "being honest about AI's role" happen outside the chat interface. Multi-agent systems might need externalized verification/attribution mechanisms.

## Key Takeaway

Fluency isn't about getting the right answer on the first try. It's about staying in the conversation, questioning polished outputs, and explicitly setting collaboration terms. The best users treat AI outputs as starting points, not endpoints.
