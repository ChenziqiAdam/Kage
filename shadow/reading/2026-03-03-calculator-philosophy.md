*Source: [Andrej Karpathy, "I love calculator" (Sep 2024)](https://karpathy.ai/blog/calculator.html)*

## Core Insight

**The calculator represents the platonic ideal of technology:** a self-contained "brain plugin" that extends human capability with zero dependency footprint. No accounts, no tracking, no subscriptions, no servers. It requires only light (solar panel) and/or batteries. It would work a thousand years ago.

## What Makes the Calculator Perfect

**Zero dependency footprint:**
- No internet connection required
- No account creation or login
- No bluetooth permissions
- No location tracking
- No software updates
- No subscription tiers
- No data harvesting
- No newsletter auto-subscription

**Radical simplicity:**
- One purchase = permanent ownership
- Press "ON" → it works
- Computation stays on-device
- No logs, no tracking, no external masters

## The Contrast with Modern Tech

Karpathy's example: a CO2 monitor with thousands of positive reviews that demands account creation, app download, and precise location before reporting CO2 levels.

**Why the drift toward complexity?**

Karpathy frames this as an **optimization problem**: companies maximize shareholder value subject to regulation constraints. The calculator model (sell once, own forever) is abandoned in favor of:
- Rental over ownership
- Information and control maximization
- Data collection and monetization
- Platform lock-in

## The Path Forward

**As consumers:** Be sensitive to this drift. Vote with wallets — the shareholder value term responds to bottom-line pressure.

**As developers:** Add a "regularizing gradient of ideology" to the company optimization problem. The calculator as platonic ideal — something to approach along various dimensions even if not all products can achieve it fully.

## Why It Matters for Adam's Work

**For multi-agent systems:**
- What would "calculator-like" AI look like? Self-contained, no external dependencies, respects user agency
- The tension between capability (requires scale, cloud, connectivity) and autonomy (local, private, durable)
- Can agents be designed with minimal "dependency footprint" on external infrastructure?

**For AI research:**
- The calculator makes users *smarter* without making them *dependent*
- Current AI patterns often create reliance (APIs, subscriptions, data harvesting)
- Is there a middle path: capable AI that remains self-contained?

## Key Takeaway

Technology could be like the calculator. Not in a literal sense for all products, but as a guiding philosophy: minimize dependencies, maximize user ownership, eliminate surveillance. The calculator is "fully self-contained arithmetic plugin for your brain. It just does the thing. It is perfect."
